# Where NASA's lunar-gravity fire data are most surprising or thinnest

This page complements [`suggestions_for_nasa.md`](suggestions_for_nasa.md). That page says **what to measure**; this one says **which materials the existing data leave most open**. It is an independent student team's reading of NASA's public data. NASA has not reviewed or endorsed it. **It is not a safety ranking**: no material here is called safe or unsafe.

## Three groups, and what would settle each

### Most surprising
- **The figure captioned P-213 (Fig. 8)**: Moon Gap +2.75 points, range +1.84 to +3.66, the only range entirely above zero. NASA itself reports, for Figs. 7-9, that the lunar-gravity limit was "less than the extrapolated value (for any assumed buoyant velocity) from the WSTF testing. This result is contrary to expected behavior" (ICES-2025-392 p. 9).
- **What would settle it:** first confirm which material that image shows (suggestions #5), then repeat it with one method difference changed at a time (suggestions #4, the matched-method lunar and WSTF repeats).

### Thinnest evidence
- **Polycarbonate (Fig. 11)**: Moon Gap −3.37, range −7.01 to +0.27 (width 7.28), which includes zero, so the sign is not settled.
- **Cotton T-shirt (smolder) (Fig. 10)**: Moon Gap −2.47, range −4.96 to +0.01 (width 4.97), which includes zero, so the sign is not settled.
- **PMMA (acrylic) (as captioned) (Fig. 7)**: Moon Gap +0.11, range −1.38 to +1.59 (width 2.97), which includes zero, so the sign is not settled.
- **What would settle it:** more repeats near each limit and the same ignition method in both facilities (suggestions #4); the lunar limit's own test-step size is not in the 95% range, so finer oxygen steps would also help.

### Moon easier than the Earth trend
- **Nomex Velcro (Fig. 12)**: Moon Gap −2.95, range −4.04 to −1.86, entirely below zero: in lunar gravity it needed more oxygen than the Earth trend extended to zero flow.
- **What would settle why:** a measured flow at the flame in lunar gravity (suggestions #1), since the Earth estimate is an extrapolation to zero flow and the lunar flow is not known.

## What NASA could test next (FM2 and beyond)

In short: confirm the identity and repeat the most surprising result, tighten the three open ranges, and measure the flow at the flame. FM2's slides say its hardware "does not support ignition approaches used at WSTF or in Centrifuge. (flow change, oxygen change)" (PDF p. 14), so some of these need other facilities too.

## Limits of this page

- The lunar-gravity limits come from about 5-second drop-tower centrifuge runs (ICES-2025-392).
- The Earth still-air estimate is a straight-line fit to 60-144 cm/s data extended to zero flow, far outside the tested range.
- No airflow is stated for the Moon: NASA reports the velocity induced by the simulated gravity as not known (ICES-2025-392 pp. 8, 10).
- The Figs. 7-9 shift above means the PMMA and P-213 rows may belong to other materials.
- An independent student team's analysis of public data, not NASA's findings.
