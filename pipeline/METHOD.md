# Measuring flame spread from NASA's BASS-II photos: method, validation, limits

Source text for the Method page, README and project page. Every number here comes from `data/derived/spread_rates.csv` or a cited source. Regenerate the numbers if the CSV changes.

## What we measured
BASS-II (Burning and Suppression of Solids II) burned black PMMA rods inside the Microgravity Science Glovebox on the ISS in 2014, with the crew changing the opposed airflow during each test. NASA's archive (PSI-25, CC0, DOI 10.60555/4qc4-de67) holds the raw photos, but its test table has no spread-rate column. We measured the rate of flame spread from the raw photos and compared it with the rates Link et al. (2018) published for the same tests.

## How
1. **Photos to light.** Each Nikon D300S raw (.NEF) frame is decoded to linear light (rawpy) and divided by its shutter time from EXIF. Aperture (f/2.0) and ISO (1000) are fixed, so this removes the exposure changes (1/8000 s to 1/3 s within one test). Time comes from EXIF DateTimeOriginal plus SubSecTimeOriginal.
2. **Flame mask.** The flame's brightness falls about 1000x during a test as the flow drops and the flame turns dim and blue, so the threshold is relative: 0.3 x each frame's own 99.9th-percentile brightness. Then a morphological opening removes hot pixels, and the largest connected region inside a band around the rod axis is kept. DSLR blue light from microgravity flames tracks CH* emission near 431 nm (Giassi et al., NTRS 20160012298).
3. **Leading edge.** For each image row, take the front-most flame pixel on the upstream side; the edge is the 90th percentile of those across rows. (A percentile over all flame pixels moves slower than the front as the flame grows. Our synthetic test caught this 1% bias.)
4. **Scale, measured not borrowed.** px/mm comes from the rod's known diameter (6.35 mm, 1/4 in.) in a well-lit full-resolution frame: 221.2 +/- 1.2 px across 11 columns, so **34.83 px/mm** (Link 2018 states 34 px/mm). NASA's rates are never used to set the scale.
5. **Flow steps.** For every test the step times come from NASA's own test video, which shows the fan setting and a GMT clock. The photo and video clocks agree within about 3 s: P19's fan burst at 13:04:04 matches a disturbance in the tracked edge at 13:04:07. Table S1's step durations were only an early fallback; no reported row uses them now. No published rate is used to place any window. We also tried finding the steps from the edge track alone, with a change-point fit (an exact dynamic-programming split into straight pieces). It isn't good enough to replace the video clock: over the 21 steps its nearest change point lands a median of 54 s from the video-read step time, and within 60 s for 12 of them, on steps that last 110 to 260 s. So each row's `notes` reports that offset as a check, and no window is ever placed from it.
6. **Rate.** Each step's rate is a least-squares slope over 25-90% of the step. That skips the airflow sensor's ~1 min lag and the jump in flame position after each flow change. The rule was frozen before the final numbers, and every row's `notes` also gives the rate under the earlier "later 75%" rule as a sensitivity check. The 95% interval is Student-t(0.975, n-2) times the slope's standard error. Threshold sensitivity is the half-range of the rate with the threshold at 0.8x and 1.2x. A fitted slope of zero or less is not physical for a spreading flame, so that step is written as unusable, with the fitted value kept in its note (rule added 2026-11-15; it never changes a positive rate).

## Validation (Link et al. 2018, Sci Rep 8:120, Supplement Table S1, p. 1)
Table S1 prints Vf x 10^3. Its right block's "(mm/s)" header is a typo for cm/s (checked against Fig. S1's edge slopes), so mm/s = printed value / 100.

| B16 (P16) | 6.35 | 18.2 | 0.72 | 0.0576 +/- 0.0033 | 0.0568 | +1.4% |
| B19 (P19) | 6.35 | 17.5 | 0.83 | 0.0617 +/- 0.0027 | 0.0625 | -1.3% |
| B19 (P19) | 6.35 | 17.5 | 0.72 | 0.0529 +/- 0.0019 | 0.0544 | -2.8% |

| P17 (P17) | 6.35 | 16.8 | 0.41 | 0.0242 +/- 0.0036 | 0.0226 | +7.1% |

**Headline: 8 low-flow segments from 4 tests (two rod sizes, 16.8-18.2% O2). Median absolute error 6.8%, all 8 within +/-15%, 6 of 8 within +/-10%, and the mean signed error is +1.1%.** The data do not detect a mean bias, but 4 tests cannot establish that bias is absent. Use this result, not the earlier "2.15%", which covered only B16 and B19.

**How certain that is (bootstrap, 20,000 resamples):** with only 8 segments the median is uncertain. Resampling whole tests (the more conservative unit, since steps of one test share a burn) gives a 95% interval of **2.1-13.2%** (`docs/research/accuracy/analysis.json`); resampling individual segments gives 1.4-13.2%. The mean signed error's 95% interval (segment resampling) is **-4.8% to +7.0%**, which includes zero, so a mean bias is not detected. (Corrected 2026-11-15: an earlier version said both resampling units gave about 2-13% and gave -4.6% for the mean interval; a re-check reproduced neither.) Say "median 6.8% (95% CI about 2-13%)" whenever space allows.

The frame-based tracking error formula (Bhattacharjee et al., Fire Safety J. 2017, via Carmignani's 2019 dissertation) is error = (1/dt)(1/(p Vf) + 1/f). An earlier 2.4-3.3% estimate for B16/B19 does not cover every observed discrepancy: B16's 0.83 cm/s step differs by 9.6%. It is a pixel and frame resolution heuristic, not a full uncertainty interval; see `docs/research/accuracy/README.md` for a stepwise diagnostic. Likely reasons for larger errors include slower spread rates (a smaller p·Vf), shorter steps, and a less certain match between steps and fan settings. Two flow labels behave differently across days: fan 41 is 0.72 cm/s on day 203 but 0.41 cm/s on day 210. Steps were matched to Table S1 rows by order and duration, never by rate.

## Limitations (shown, not hidden)
- **Early high-flow steps are unreliable.** For 7.55 and 1.67 cm/s, both tests disagree with Table S1 (-16% to -50%). Most of these rows have 95% intervals of 30-50% of the rate and are flagged automatically. B16 at 7.55 cm/s (-20%) has a tighter interval and so isn't flagged, but it shows the same bias and should not be quoted as validated either. These steps come first, in the flame's bright early phase, while its bright, short front jitters between frames (B13b below shows that coming right after ignition isn't the cause by itself). The other early high-flow rows are flagged "LOW CONFIDENCE" in the CSV. A likely cause shows in the overlay videos: in those bright early frames the relative threshold locks onto the bright core, and a faint blue leading zone extends about 8 mm further upstream (B16 frame 61). A constant offset doesn't change a slope, but one that changes over time does. The threshold rule was left frozen rather than tuned to fix this.
- **The same pattern holds in the new tests.** B12's 1.01 and 0.82 cm/s steps (-67%, +93%) are flagged. P17's 1.01 cm/s step (-23%) isn't flagged by the CI rule but swings +/-48% with the threshold, so it's unreliable too. In B12 the threshold sometimes drops the dim blue front under a bright sooty core: the same front gives tracked edges about 9 mm apart in frames 79 and 80.
- **A fifth test, B13b (12.7 mm rod, 20.6% O2), changes the explanation.** Its first step, at 1.64 cm/s and right after ignition, matches Table S1 within +5.8% (0.0750 vs 0.0709 mm/s). Its fit window falls entirely in the dim blue phase. So neither high flow nor being the first step is the cause by itself. The failures track the **bright early phase**, where the brightness threshold locks onto the flame's core, which agrees with the blue-channel diagnostic. B13b's second step (1.54 cm/s) has no usable frames under the frozen 3000 px area gate (the dim flame on the 12.7 mm rod is 1,400-1,900 px). With a 1000 px gate, chosen from the gate areas without looking at any rate, it gives -4.1%, which is reported here only as a sensitivity result. B13b's scale (32.83 px/mm) comes from plain gradient edges, because its re-burned, bubbled rod has no top highlight: about +/-3% systematic uncertainty from the rod's taper. B13b stays out of the validated headline because both its steps are above 1 cm/s.
- **A sixth test, B13a (the same rod's first burn, 20.7% O2), mostly fails, and we report it.** Of its four steps (1.64, 1.54, 1.43, 1.38 cm/s), only one gives a rate: 1.54 cm/s, 0.0573 +/- 0.0059 mm/s against Table S1's 0.0527 (+8.7%), from just 6 photos, and the earlier "later 75%" rule gives -15.6% for it, so it's weak. The 1.64 cm/s step falls in the bright phase and its fitted slope is negative (-0.0127 mm/s), which isn't physical, so no rate is reported. The last two steps have no photos that pass the frozen 3000 px area gate. B13a's flow steps are also the least certain we used: the fan display doesn't change between them, so they were placed from how often the display flickers, checked against the crew's photo bursts. Scale 32.00 px/mm from gradient edges, with about +/-3.5% systematic uncertainty. All of B13a is above 1 cm/s, so none of it is in the headline.
- **Four tests in the headline.** Link's data "were not systematically taken". P19's rod burned to the holder, so P19 reuses P16's rod calibration (same day, same camera). B12 and P17 each have their own calibration: 34.18 and 33.70 px/mm. B9 (1.27 cm), B22, B10 and C25 were tried and dropped: their flow steps can't be read from a video overlay. B10 has no video at all. C25's five pot changes all sit inside one steady FAN 41 reading for 720 s.
- **Independent tool cross-check (Flame Tracker).** We ran the unmodified front-detection code of the open-source Flame Tracker (Carmignani, SoftwareX 15:100791, 2021; GPL-3.0, run from a separate clone, not vendored) on B19's two validated steps, with its default settings: 0.0606 and 0.0535 mm/s, against our 0.0617 and 0.0529 (-1.8%, +1.1%) and Table S1's 0.0625 and 0.0544. The two edges correlate at r = 0.996 and 0.993 frame by frame. Caveats: only the edge detection is independent (the scale, windows and fit rule are ours), and the first export (gamma-encoded) gave a stuck edge and no usable rate. We switched to a linear export because of that stuck track, not because of any rate. Details: `docs/research/flametracker_crosscheck.md`.
- **Scope of Link 2018's "no flame spread below 0.6 cm/s".** The paper says "No flame spread was observed at flow velocities less than 0.6 cm/s" (Results, text on Fig. 6, which shows B16 and B19 at 0.64 cm). That sentence describes those two tests. It isn't a limit for every BASS-II test: Table S1 itself lists spread at 0.48 cm/s (B12, 0.95 cm rod, 0.0237 mm/s) and 0.41 cm/s (P17, 0.64 cm rod, 0.0226 mm/s), and our tracker measured both steps.
- **Condition labels are approximate (this doesn't change any rate or the comparison with Table S1, which is the same flame).**
  - **Oxygen:** each test's O2 label is its pre-burn reading. NASA's PSI-25 experimental table gives a calibrated initial and final O2 per test, and Table S1's O2 equals the initial value. O2 then fell during every burn, by 0.3 to 1.6 points in our six tests, and was read only "before and after each BASS-II test" (Olson, Ferkul et al., ICES-2015-196, p. 5).
  - **Airflow:** the low-flow airflow labels are the least certain condition. The fan was "calibrated with the air velocity transducer at the end of every operations day" (ICES-2015-196, p. 3). In NASA's fan calibration file, the fan display used for our low-flow steps (about 42) read 0 to 3.75 cm/s across calibration days. During the steps themselves, the test videos' on-screen air sensor (whole cm/s only) reads 0 in the 0.83, 0.72, 0.60, 0.48 and 0.41 cm/s steps and in all B13a/B13b steps, 2 in both 0.82 cm/s steps (B12, P17), and about 8 in the 7.55 cm/s steps (`docs/research/accuracy/air_display/`), so it can't confirm or refute the low-flow labels.
  - **Who should care:** anyone comparing these rates with a model or a habitat condition. Details: `docs/research/accuracy/uncertainty_floor.md`.

## Diagnostic: why the high-flow steps fail (not used for any reported rate)
The method above is frozen. To understand its failures we re-tracked every step with ONE change: the edge found on the **blue channel alone** (CH* emission near 431 nm) instead of mean brightness, with the same windows, scale, threshold and 25-90% fit (`pipeline/diagnostics/blue_edge.py`, results in `docs/research/blue_edge_diagnostic.json`).
- **B12 and P17 near 1 cm/s are a tracker problem, not a flame problem.** With the blue-channel edge, B12 1.01 cm/s goes from -66.9% to +10.3%, B12 0.82 cm/s from +93.3% to -0.4%, and P17 1.01 cm/s from -23.1% to +9.0%. A bright, sooty core outshines the blue front, so a brightness threshold locks onto the core.
- **B16/B19's first high-flow steps (7.55, 1.67 cm/s) fail with either edge** (-39% to -100%, or too few frames). They fall in the bright early phase (see B13b above), so changing the edge definition alone doesn't rescue them.
- **The low-flow steps agree under both definitions** (B16 0.72: +1.4% vs +0.8%; B19 0.72: -2.8% vs -2.4%; P17 0.41: +7.1% vs +7.1%). The validated results don't depend on that choice. B12's dimmest late steps are too faint for a blue-only edge.
We did not switch methods after seeing which agrees better with NASA, because that would make the validation circular.

## A fused edge (v2), tested under a rule fixed in advance: not adopted
The obvious next version takes the front-most of the brightness edge and the blue-channel edge. Before measuring any new test we wrote down the rule (`pipeline/PREREGISTRATION.md`, committed 2026-11-14): low-flow steps stay on v1 for good, and v2 replaces v1 for steps at or above 1 cm/s only if its median error is smaller on held-out tests, meaning tests not used to design it.
- **Held-out tests:** B13b and B13a. Median |error| over the held-out steps with a rate: **v1 8.7%, v2 11.0% (n = 3)**. v2 didn't win, so **v1 stays for every reported rate**. Leaving out B13a's non-physical 1.64 cm/s step gives 7.3% against 8.2% (n = 2), the same decision.
- **What it means:** three steps from two burns of one rod is too little to say v2 is worse in general. It does show the fused edge isn't a quick fix: 4 of the 6 held-out steps have no usable rate under either version.
- **Where the blue-light edge still appears:** as a dashed line labelled "diagnostic" in the overlay videos, so viewers can see the blue front the brightness edge misses in the bright early phase. It's never used for a rate.
